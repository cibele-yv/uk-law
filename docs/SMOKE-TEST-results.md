> "Where— (a) the recipient has not given notice requesting a hearing in respect of the offence ... and (b) the fixed penalty has not been paid ... before the end of the suspended enforcement period, a sum equal to the fixed penalty plus one–half of the amount of that penalty may be registered under section 71 of this Act for enforcement against the recipient as a fine."

- within fourteen days "a notice of the intended prosecution specifying the nature of the alleged offence and the time and place where it is alleged to have been committed" was served on him or on the registered keeper of the vehicle.

Source: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/53/section/1/data.xml

This is not a loophole you can rely on, for three reasons:
- Section 1(3) says the requirement is "deemed to have been complied with unless and until the contrary is proved."
- Section 1(2) says a notice sent by registered post or recorded delivery to your last known address counts as served, "notwithstanding that the notice was returned as undelivered or was for any other reason not received by him."
- Under section 2 (in force as at 2022-11-30), the requirement doesn't apply if an accident occurred because of the vehicle. It also doesn't apply if a fixed penalty notice was given. Section 2(3) says a failure isn't a bar to conviction if "the accused by his own conduct contributed to the failure." That would cover giving a false address or ignoring correspondence.

Source: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/53/section/2/data.xml
