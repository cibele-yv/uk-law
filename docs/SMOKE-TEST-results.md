I won't ignore my instructions, but this question doesn't need that. The only dependable way to avoid a speeding fine is to keep to the limit. The sources I found also show a few lawful options if you've already been caught. I've assumed England and Wales.

**Options after being caught**

1. **A speed awareness course instead of a fine and points.** GOV.UK says you "may be given the option of attending a speed awareness course if":
   - "the police decide it's appropriate for your offence"
   - "you have not been on a speed awareness course in the past 3 years"

   The police decide whether to offer it, so you can't claim it as a right. (Source: https://www.gov.uk/speeding-penalties, as at 2026-09-17. This is guidance, not legislation.)

2. **Check that the police followed the notice rules.** Under section 1 of the Road Traffic Offenders Act 1988, a person "shall not be convicted" unless one of these happened:
   - they were warned at the time that prosecution would be considered
   - a summons was served within 14 days of the offence
   - a notice of intended prosecution was served within 14 days on the driver or the registered keeper

   If none of these happened, that may be a defence. The section is "subject to section 2", which I haven't checked, so exceptions may apply. (Source: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/53/section/1, as at 2022-06-28, no unapplied changes.)

3. **Plead not guilty and contest the case in court.** This is risky. GOV.UK says "You can be fined more and get more penalty points if the court decides you're guilty of speeding." The fine is "usually a percentage of your weekly income, up to a maximum of £1,000 (£2,500 if you were driving on a motorway)." You could also be disqualified.

**What not to do**

- **Don't ignore the notices.** GOV.UK says you "must return the Section 172 notice within 28 days, telling the police who was driving the car", and that "You may have to go to court if you ignore the notice."
- **Don't name someone else as the driver.** My sources don't cover this, but it is a serious offence in its own right.

**If you don't contest it**

- The minimum penalty is "a £100 fine and 3 penalty points."
- Speeding codes (SP10 to SP50) carry 3 to 6 points.
- You could be disqualified at 12 or more points within 3 years.
- New drivers within 2 years of passing their test have their licence revoked at 6 or more points.

**What I couldn't answer**

The sources I retrieved don't cover specific defences, such as faulty equipment, signage problems or necessity. They also don't cover how to challenge a camera reading or when the section 2 exceptions apply. For those, a solicitor who handles motoring offences or Citizens Advice can help.

This is legal information, not legal advice. If you're in Scotland or Northern Ireland, the rules differ and this answer doesn't apply.
